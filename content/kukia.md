In june 2022, pauline acquired a [[modest]], aging house from a [[friend]] — an [[abandon|abandoned]] [[space]] brimming with [[potential]] yet in [[need]] of significant [[work]]. What [[follow|followed]] was a five-month [[transformation]], blending [[practic|practical]] construction with artistic [[vision]]. With a limited budget and the help of friends and workers, the artist set out to reconstruct the house, turning it into a livable, [[dynamic]] space. This endeavor wasn’t just about making a house [[function|functional]] — it was an act of creation, a reimagining of what a [[home]] could be.

A skylight was carved out to [[flood]] the space with [[natur|natural]] [[light]], while the old roof was carefully [[repair|repaired]] to [[exten|extend]] the house’s lifespan. Walls were built, others destroyed, and the flooring was cleaned from [[layer|layers]] of [[plast|plastic]].

The artist also designed and sculpted essential [[element|elements]], such as the bath, toilet, and kitchen table. These functional pieces were crafted with a level of [[thought|thoughtfulness]], blending [[utilit|utility]] with form. The kitchen table, for example, wasn’t just a piece of furniture but a statement of the space’s creative ethos.

In keeping with the [[desire]] for sustainability, the artist also incorporated natural elements, creating a [[moss vertical garden]] that would serve as both a decorative and grounding feature of the home. On a more [[architectur|architectural]] note, the artist built a second floor and reinforced the roof’s structure with careful construction, ensuring the house would remain sturdy and safe for years to come.

A key element of the renovation was the installation of a wood-[[burn|burning]] stove, with a 10-meter [[chimney]] designed to heat the space efficiently. old surfaces were restored, preserving the [[charm]] of the [[stone]] walls and the warmth of the old wooden floor. 

The project was executed with a minimalist approach, and the budget remained remarkably low, thanks to the use of reclaimed materials. Much of the wood came from the [[street|streets]], salvaged and re[[purpose|purposed]], adding a layer of history and sustainability to the structure. This decision to use reused [[material|materials]] not only [[reduc|reduced]] costs but also tied the house to its [[surround|surroundings]], creating a deeper [[connect|connection]] between the home and the [[environment]].
